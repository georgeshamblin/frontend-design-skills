# Copy That Sounds Human

## When to Use
- Writing or reviewing any text a visitor will read
- ALWAYS as part of the final page review — the words are the loudest AI tell

## Rule one: no dash as punctuation

Do not use a dash to splice thoughts together unless it is quite literally
necessary. Em dashes and their double hyphen stand-ins are the single most
recognizable marker of generated prose.

- Bad: `We handle your books — so you can focus on your business — year round.`
- Good: `We handle your books all year, so you can run your business.`

Rewrite every dash as one of: a period and a new sentence, a comma, a colon, or the
word the dash was hiding ("because", "which means", "including").

Hyphens inside genuinely hyphenated words are fine when grammar requires them
(self-employed, year-end filing). When a natural rewording exists, prefer it. Ranges
in tables and phone numbers keep their hyphens; this rule is about prose.

## Rule two: the banned list

These words and shapes mark text as generated. Do not ship them:

- **Words:** seamless, elevate, unlock, empower, unleash, supercharge, effortless,
  game-changing, cutting-edge, robust, holistic, streamline, leverage (as a verb),
  solutions (as a category), "in today's fast-paced world"
- **Shapes:** "It's not just X, it's Y." / "Whether you're A or B, we've got you
  covered." / "Look no further." / rhetorical question openers ("Tired of high
  taxes?") / three punchy fragments in a row ("Fast. Simple. Done.")
- **Structure:** a heading that promises and a subline that repeats the promise in
  more words. Say it once, better.

## Rule three: concrete beats impressive

Every sentence must survive the question "could a competitor say this word for
word?" If yes, replace it with something only this business can say.

- Bad: `Expert accounting services tailored to your needs.`
- Good: `Tax preparation and bookkeeping for contractors and farms in the
  Sacramento Valley.`

Numbers, places, service names, prices, and deadlines are the words that sell.
Adjectives are the words that fill space.

## Voice

- Write like the owner explaining to a neighbor: short sentences, everyday words,
  contractions welcome.
- Buttons say what happens: "Book a call", "Get the checklist". Never "Submit",
  never "Learn More" when something specific fits.
- Error and empty states get the same care as headlines; that is where trust is won.
- Read the final copy ALOUD. Anywhere you would not say the sentence to a person's
  face, rewrite it.

## Truth guardrails

Never invent testimonials, statistics, credentials, awards, or years in business.
Never use a regulated title (CPA, attorney, licensed anything) without written
confirmation. Charm is worthless if one checkable claim is false.
