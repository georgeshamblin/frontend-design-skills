# Beautiful Heroes

## When to Use
- Building the top of any landing or home page
- The hero "looks like every other site" in review

## What a hero must do

One screen, three answers, five seconds: what is this, who is it for, what do I do
next. Everything else in the hero exists to serve those or gets cut.

## The headline

The headline is the page's biggest design element AND its most important copy. Both
disciplines apply (see copy-that-sounds-human):

- Name the specific value for the specific customer. `Tax preparation and planning
  for contractors and farms in the Sacramento Valley` beats `Modern Accounting
  Solutions for Your Business` in every measurable way.
- 4 to 12 words. If it needs more, the extra words belong in the subline.
- The subline adds NEW information: who it serves, what makes it different, a
  concrete proof point. Never a rephrase of the headline.
- Typography does the drama: hero size from the clamp in typography-that-reads,
  tight leading, `text-wrap: balance`. No gradient text, no word in a different
  color unless the brand truly owns that move.

## Composition patterns that do not look generated

Pick ONE, driven by what real assets exist:

1. **Left aligned text, strong right visual.** Text column 5/12, real photo or
   product shot 7/12. The workhorse. Needs a genuinely good image.
2. **Centered statement, no image.** Big type on generous air, one button. Works
   when the words are strong and the brand is typographic. The whole screen is
   typography, so the details must be perfect.
3. **Full bleed image, text on a scrim.** Only with a real, high quality photo of
   the actual place, team, or work. Gradient scrim for contrast (4.5:1 minimum).
   Stock photography kills this pattern instantly.
4. **Product first.** Screenshot or mockup dominating, headline above. For software.

The generated look is pattern 2's layout with pattern 1's decoration: centered text
PLUS floating blobs, badge pills, and a fake screenshot. Choose one pattern and
commit.

## Calls to action

- One primary button, verb first, specific: `Book a free consultation`. Optionally
  one quiet secondary link (`See services`). Two equal buttons split the click.
- Under or near the button, one line of friction reducer if TRUE: real hours, real
  response time, real pricing note. Nothing invented.

## Trust, if it exists

Real proof only: actual client logos with permission, a true years in business
number, a real review count. An empty hero is better than a fabricated trust bar;
fake proof is the tell that costs the sale AND creates liability.

## Verify

Load the page at 1440px and 375px. At both sizes: headline fully visible without
scrolling, one dominant element (squint test), button tappable, image not cropping
its subject. Then read the headline aloud to someone who has never seen the
business; if they cannot say back what the company does, rewrite it.
